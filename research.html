// Research Themes

Four lines of work that keep converging on the same question: when is an estimate actually trustworthy?

01 — Causal Inference

Nonparametric treatment effect estimation

Bayesian Causal Forests and their extensions: what the propensity-score component actually buys you, how to make difference-in-differences work under staggered adoption and effect heterogeneity, and how to estimate ATE and CATE when the treatment is continuous rather than binary.

02 — Methodology

How we evaluate causal estimators

Simulation studies in causal inference are usually built by convention rather than by design, which quietly biases every comparison drawn from them. This line of work asks how many replications are actually needed, what a data-generating process should look like, and what an ablation study can and cannot establish.

03 — Topological Data Analysis

Shape as a statistical summary

Persistent homology applied to financial markets — the Topological Tail Dependence Theory — and, more recently, the question of what topology means once causal assumptions are on the table. Persistence diagrams, Wasserstein and bottleneck distances, and stability results as inputs to forecasting and to causal estimands.

04 — Agentic & Financial AI

Causal reasoning inside AI systems

Deep architectures for realized volatility (NBEATSx, NHITS, TimesNet, TSMixer), distribution-aware loss functions built on Wasserstein distance, and the current work at Dell on where agentic AI, causal inference, and causal reinforcement learning meet.

// Latest Preprint

New · 2026

A Mathematical Framework for Topological Causal Data Analysis

Hugo Gobato Souto, Ioannis Diamantis

Many modern outcomes, including images, point clouds, networks, and spatial fields, are structured objects for which \(Y^1 - Y^0\) may be undefined or scientifically inadequate. We introduce Topological Causal Data Analysis (TCDA), a framework separating the observation space, causal-model class, topological representation, and causal query. Topology does not define interventions; it supplies stable, shape-sensitive summaries after causal assumptions have been specified. We distinguish outcome-level TCDA, which transforms individual potential outcomes, from distribution-level TCDA, which transforms interventional outcome laws, and characterize when outcome and distribution level contrasts agree. Building on recent outcome-level theory, we formulate identification and doubly robust representations for Banach-space-valued summaries. At the distribution level, we identify targets through the standard causal \(g\)-formula and derive stability-transfer bounds and plug-in consistency. We also place target-specific topological ignorability within the framework, clarifying when a covariate-standardized coarse effect can be identified without identifying the full interventional laws. Finally, we delimit the role of observational topology in causal discovery: it can assist diagnosis on restricted model classes but cannot by itself identify causal structure.

// Master's Dissertation

Institute of Mathematical and Computer Sciences (ICMC), University of São Paulo · defended 2025, ahead of schedule

Advancing Robust and Reliable Estimation of Heterogeneous Treatment Effects: Methodological Innovations and Critical Evaluations

Supervised by Prof. Francisco Louzada Neto. Roughly 180 pages setting out three separate methodological contributions to the estimation of heterogeneous treatment effects.

Contribution 1

Ablation studies for nonparametric causal models

An empirical investigation showing that the propensity-score component of the Bayesian Causal Forest can be omitted without loss of performance, while cutting computational cost by roughly 21%. The result was re-tested at higher dimensionality, varied treatment-effect magnitudes, and varied sample sizes after an independent researcher challenged it. It held.

Contribution 2

TISCA — Test-Informed Simulation Count Algorithm

A principled way to determine how many replications a simulation study actually needs in order to support the comparisons it draws, replacing the arbitrary round numbers the field tends to default to.

Contribution 3

DiD-BCF — Difference-in-Differences Bayesian Causal Forest

A nonparametric estimator handling staggered adoption and treatment-effect heterogeneity in a single framework, recovering average, group-level, and conditional effects. Applied to U.S. minimum-wage policy, it uncovered conditional treatment effects that established benchmarks had missed.
